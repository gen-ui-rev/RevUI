Why Stories Make New Words Easier to Remember

Context gives unfamiliar vocabulary more ways to stay connected in memory — and more paths back to it later.

Memory follows meaning

A word list asks your brain to store isolated facts. A story links each word to a place, an action, and a reason it matters.

When you meet the word again, those details become retrieval cues. A two-sentence scene can beat repeated copying.

Retell your scene aloud the next day without looking at it.

Practice prompt

Use “hesitate,” “glimpse,” and “relieved” in one connected scene.
